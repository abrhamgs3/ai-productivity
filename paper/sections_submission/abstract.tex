\begin{abstract}
This paper examines whether AI adoption is associated with macroeconomic performance in a panel of 193 sovereign countries. The empirical design combines country fixed effects with a pre-specified robustness portfolio, including two-way fixed effects, trimmed-sample estimation, lagged AI regressors, alternative clustering, and Driscoll-Kraay inference. In the baseline specification, a 1 percent increase in AI adoption is associated with an estimated 0.025 percent increase in total factor productivity (TFP), while the corresponding association with contemporaneous GDP growth is positive but smaller. These patterns remain directionally stable across principal specification checks.

Beyond this pre-specified portfolio, four extended falsification checks qualify the headline interpretation. Decomposing the AI proxy shows that a digital-infrastructure-only sub-index is positively and significantly associated with TFP, while an innovation/patent-only sub-index is not, indicating that the association reflects general digital-capacity diffusion rather than AI-specific activity. A reverse-causality check finds that lagged TFP positively predicts current AI adoption, tempering a one-directional timing interpretation. A coverage-restricted re-estimation confirms the association survives in countries with sustained AI-data coverage, but these countries are systematically richer, more human-capital-intensive, and better governed than the rest of the panel.

The contribution is empirical as well as methodological. Methodologically, the paper provides a transparent and reproducible workflow from data validation to manuscript-ready outputs. Interpretation is intentionally conservative: estimates are presented as robust conditional associations between digital/AI capacity and productivity, not as definitive or AI-specific causal effects. Full model outputs and extended diagnostics are reported in a separate online appendix.
\end{abstract}
